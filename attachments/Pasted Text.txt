Yes. If we keep email as the primary universal source, there are several ways to enrich it substantially without turning the product into an integration nightmare.

Google could be an enormous second source

If someone uses Gmail, there’s a decent chance they also use YouTube.

YouTube officially maintains watch history, search history, likes, dislikes, subscriptions, “Not interested” feedback and other recommendation signals. Google itself says those signals are used for recommendations. 

Imagine importing:

searched “Life movie trailer”
watched Life trailer
watched “10 best alien movies”
watched Project Hail Mary trailer
watched Annihilation ending explained
watched Dune Part Three trailer

Even without Netflix history, that tells our model an enormous amount about what the person is interested in.

Google Takeout allows users to export account data, including YouTube-related information and account activity. 

So I’d have:

Connect Google

And underneath that connection, Thorbis-style progressive disclosure:

✓ Email entertainment history
✓ YouTube interests
✓ YouTube likes
✓ Google activity
✓ Movie purchases

The user doesn’t need to understand five different imports.

⸻

Then offer a “Supercharge my recommendations” button

This is where we can support the enthusiasts without burdening normal people.

Netflix

Netflix actually gives users an official Download All viewing-history CSV for each profile. That’s real watched data rather than inferred data. 

Upload it once.

Done.

Letterboxd

Excellent signal because it contains intentional watched status, ratings, reviews, diary entries and lists. Letterboxd provides an account export containing CSVs. 

Interestingly, I would not depend on their API. Letterboxd currently says API access isn’t being granted for recommendation/data-analysis projects like this. Their export is the safer path. 

Trakt

This one is particularly attractive.

Trakt exposes history through its API and has the concept of scrobbling, where media software automatically reports start/pause/stop viewing events. 

So:

Connect Trakt

could be one of our few actual integrations.

The streamlined product I’d build

Normal onboarding stays ridiculously simple:

Let’s learn what you like.

Connect Google

We’ll find movies you’ve purchased, rented, seen in theaters, and shown interest in.

Continue with Google

That’s it.

Behind that one button, email gives us the historical transactional evidence.

Then optionally:

Improve my recommendations →

Netflix history
Trakt
Letterboxd
IMDb
Other data export

And from that point forward, your own app behavior becomes the primary source of truth.

That gives you the universal nature you’re after without requiring users to install extensions, run Plex servers, connect seven streaming services, or manually enter 500 movies.

And I think there’s an important product principle here: don’t chase 100% reconstruction of someone’s past. Get enough historical evidence to make the first 10 recommendations good, then make every interaction improve recommendation #11 onward.
